# Week 12 — Challenges

Two challenges, harder and more open-ended than the exercises, both continuing directly from your own capstone idea. Where the exercises had a fixed set of tasks and a checklist of expected outcomes, these ask you to make judgment calls with real tension in them — the kind you can't fully checklist your way through, which is exactly why they're worth doing before you write the mini-project's launch narrative.

## What's different about challenge-level work

Every prior challenge in this course gave you a planted scenario to reason through. These two ask you to build the scenario yourself, from your own PRD and roadmap, and then reason through it — closer to what a real Week-1-on-the-job PM does than any single exercise this course has offered. Expect to spend real time thinking before you write, not just executing a known pattern.

| Challenge | What it asks | Depends on |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| 1 — Capstone Experiment Plan | Design a real A/B or pre/post experiment for one bet in your roadmap, with sample size and a pre-registered decision rule | Exercise 2's roadmap, Exercise 3's dashboard schema |
| 2 — Capstone Launch and Defense | Write your launch narrative and defend it against five adversarial, planted stakeholder questions | Challenge 1's experiment plan, all prior exercises |

## Before you start

Have Exercise 2's `roadmap.md` and Exercise 3's dashboard files open — both challenges reference them directly rather than introducing new scenarios from scratch.
