# Exercise 1 — Rewrite Leading Interview Questions

**Goal:** Get the Mom Test into your fingers. Read a leading question, name exactly why it fails, and rewrite it into something a real interview could use. By the end, spotting a leading question in someone else's script should feel automatic.

**Estimated time:** 45 minutes.

## Setup

No database needed for this one — just a text file. Create `rewrites.md` and put each answer under a `## Question N` heading.

## Tasks

For each question below, do three things:

1. **Name the failure** — is it hypothetical/future-focused, does it presuppose an answer, is it double-barreled, does it pitch a solution, or some combination?
2. **Rewrite it** into a Mom-Test-clean question that asks about a specific past event or observable behavior.
3. **One sentence** on what real answer your rewrite could surface that the original couldn't.

All questions are about Shiftly (the shift-scheduling app from this week's lectures) — imagine you're interviewing a shift worker or manager.

1. "Would you use a one-tap shift-swap feature if we built it?"
2. "Don't you find the current approval process really slow?"
3. "How much would you pay for a tool that made swapping shifts easier?"
4. "Do you think a marketplace of open shifts would help people on your team?"
5. "Is the app fast and easy to use?"
6. "Wouldn't it be great if you didn't need manager approval for every swap?"
7. "How often do you always struggle to find someone to cover a shift?"
8. "If we added a feature to see everyone's availability, would that solve your problem?"
9. "Do you like using text messages to coordinate shift coverage, or would you prefer something better?"
10. "You'd probably use this way more than the current process, right?"

## Worked example (do it like this)

> **Original:** "Would you use a one-tap shift-swap feature if we built it?"
> **Failure:** Hypothetical future behavior — invites an imagined, overly positive answer with zero cost to the participant for saying yes.
> **Rewrite:** "Tell me about the last time you needed someone to cover your shift. Walk me through exactly what you did, step by step."
> **What it surfaces:** The real, current process (texting? asking in person? a group chat?), how long it actually took, and where it broke down — evidence a hypothetical "would you use X" could never produce.

## Done when…

- [ ] All 10 questions have a named failure, a rewrite, and a one-sentence justification.
- [ ] None of your 10 rewrites mention a specific solution (no "marketplace," "one-tap," "feature" in the rewritten question itself).
- [ ] Every rewrite asks about something that already happened, or something directly observable, not a hypothetical future.
- [ ] At least 3 of your rewrites include a natural follow-up "why" you'd ask next, based on Lecture 2's laddering technique.

## Stretch

- Question 9 has an extra flaw beyond being leading — it's also a forced-choice, false-dilemma framing ("like this, or want something better"). Rewrite it a second way that avoids that trap too.
- Pick your two favorite rewrites and write the single best follow-up question you'd ask depending on how the participant answers — show the branch for two different plausible answers.
