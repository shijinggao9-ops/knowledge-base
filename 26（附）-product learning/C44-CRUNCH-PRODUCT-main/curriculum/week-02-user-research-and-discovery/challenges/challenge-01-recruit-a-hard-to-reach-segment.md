# Challenge 1 — Recruit a Hard-to-Reach Segment

**Time:** ~60 minutes. **Difficulty:** Medium. **No single right answer.**

## The scenario

Shiftly's interviews so far (this week's seed data) skew toward participants recruited via `in_app_banner`, `manager_referral`, and a general `panel` — and they skew toward day and swing shifts. Your VP of Product asks a sharp question: **"We haven't talked to a single overnight warehouse worker. Is their experience even the same? Go find out."**

Overnight warehouse shift workers are a genuinely hard-to-reach segment for reasons that aren't your fault but are absolutely your problem:

- They're asleep or working during your normal business hours — an `in_app_banner` sent at 2pm will never reach someone whose shift is midnight–8am.
- A `manager_referral` is suspect here: the manager who refers them is also the person overnight workers may be least comfortable being fully honest about (recall this week's `manager_bottleneck` theme — if the bottleneck *is* the manager, a manager-selected sample will systematically miss or soften that finding).
- Turnover tends to be higher on overnight shifts, so your participant pool is smaller and changes faster than for day-shift staff.
- Many overnight workers are balancing a second job or caregiving during the day, so a scheduled daytime interview slot may simply not exist for them.

## Your task

Write a recruiting plan in `challenge-01.md` that addresses all of the following:

1. **Recruiting channel(s).** Name at least two ways you'd actually reach overnight warehouse workers that don't rely on their manager choosing who gets asked. Be concrete — "post in the break room" is more useful than "use social media."
2. **Screener questions.** Write 4–6 screening questions (not interview questions — screening questions decide *who gets in*, before the interview even starts) that would correctly identify a genuine overnight warehouse worker and exclude someone who just says yes to get an incentive. Include at least one question designed to catch a "professional survey-taker" trying to qualify dishonestly.
3. **Sample composition and bias check.** State a target sample size and composition (e.g., how many workers vs. leads, how many different warehouses/sites, tenure spread) and explain **one specific way** your own recruiting channel could still bias who you reach — then say what you'd do to counter it. (Every channel has a bias; a strong answer names its own, rather than pretending the plan is neutral.)
4. **Incentive design.** Propose a fair incentive for this segment, and explain one thing you'd deliberately avoid (e.g., an incentive too large relative to their hourly wage can attract people motivated by the money rather than genuine participants, distorting your sample).
5. **Scheduling accommodation.** Given that this segment's "daytime" is often their sleep window, propose how you'd actually schedule interviews — and be honest if this means the interview format itself (live call vs. async voice memo vs. text-based) might need to change for this segment specifically.

## Constraints

- You cannot rely on a manager to hand-pick who you talk to — state explicitly how your plan avoids that.
- Your plan must reach **at least 3 different overnight shift workers from at least 2 different warehouse sites** — a single site's overnight crew could have a site-specific culture that isn't representative.
- Assume a modest budget: incentives, but no travel budget to visit sites in person.

## Hints

<details>
<summary>On avoiding the manager-referral trap</summary>

Consider recruiting via a channel overnight workers control themselves — an opt-in QR code posted in the break room or on time-clock terminals they physically pass (not requiring app engagement, since usage patterns may differ from day shift), or an anonymous-feeling all-staff message from HR/research rather than their direct manager, explicitly stating responses won't be shared with their manager.

</details>

<details>
<summary>On the screener catching dishonest qualifiers</summary>

A good screener question asks for a specific, hard-to-fake detail a genuine overnight worker would know without thinking (e.g., "what time does your shift typically start and end?" cross-checked against a follow-up like "what's the first task you usually do when you clock in?") rather than a simple yes/no ("are you an overnight worker? Y/N") that anyone can answer correctly by guessing what you want to hear.

</details>

## How success is judged

| Signal | Weak answer | Strong answer |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| Channel independence | Relies on manager to select participants | Uses a channel workers control themselves |
| Screener rigor | Yes/no self-report only | Includes a detail-based cross-check question |
| Bias self-awareness | Claims the plan is neutral | Names a specific bias in its own chosen channel and a mitigation |
| Incentive judgment | Picks a number with no reasoning | Explains the risk of over- or under-incentivizing this segment |
| Scheduling realism | Assumes a normal 9–5 interview slot works | Adapts the format to this segment's actual clock |
