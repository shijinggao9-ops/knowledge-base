# Mini-Project — Teardown of a Product You Use

> Pick one real product you use. Tear it apart: who's the PM function serving, who are its users, what job do they hire it for, what's its value proposition, what lifecycle stage is it in, and what bets is its team actually making right now. One product, one clean deliverable, every Week 1 skill in one document.

**Estimated time:** 2.5–3 hours, best done Saturday after the exercises and challenges.

This is the week's capstone. A great PM can walk up to almost any product — one they've never worked on — and produce this exact analysis inside an hour, using nothing but the product itself, its public presence, and disciplined judgment. That's the skill this project builds. You'll do it once, thoroughly, this week; you'll get faster at it every week for the rest of the course.

---

## Deliverable

A directory in your portfolio `c44-week-01/mini-project/` containing:

1. `teardown.md` — the full analysis (structure below).
2. `evidence/` — a folder with anything you gathered: screenshots, review excerpts, pricing snapshots, links. Every claim in `teardown.md` that isn't common knowledge should trace back to something in here.
3. `notes.md` — a short reflection (see the end).

## Pick your product

Choose one product you use regularly and can research honestly — reuse the one from Exercise 1 if you'd like a head start, or pick a fresh one. It should be:

- **Real and specific** — not "social media," but one named app (Instagram, not "a social app").
- **Something you can observe directly** — you should be able to use it yourself, not rely entirely on secondhand descriptions.
- **At least 18 months old** — new products don't yet have enough visible history for the lifecycle section.

---

## `teardown.md` structure

### Part 1 — The role (Lecture 1)

- In one paragraph: based on what you can observe (release notes, public roadmap, hiring pages if visible, how the product evolves), what does the PM function for this product seem to actually own versus influence? You won't have insider knowledge — reason from evidence, and say explicitly where you're inferring versus where you're certain.

### Part 2 — Users, job, and value (Lecture 2)

- 2–3 segments (traits, not jobs yet).
- One persona, built from real behavior you can observe or plausibly infer — not invented.
- One JTBD statement for that persona, in the full template, that passes the "is this a disguised feature request?" test.
- A pains/gains → pain relievers/gain creators table, at least 3 rows each.
- One clean value proposition sentence — specific, checkable, no slogans.

### Part 3 — Lifecycle stage (Lecture 3)

- Diagnose the product's current lifecycle stage.
- Cite **at least three independent pieces of evidence** (reuse the evidence-gathering approach from Challenge 2 if you did it on this same product — pricing history, changelog cadence, review trends, hiring signals, news).
- State one competing interpretation of your evidence and why you still land where you do.

### Part 4 — The VVF+U lens on a real bet

- Identify **one specific recent change** the product actually shipped (a real feature, redesign, or pricing change you can point to — not hypothetical).
- Run the full VVF+U lens on it (Lecture 3, Section 3), scoring or reasoning through all four dimensions.
- State what you believe the team's strategic bet was behind this change, in the claim/reason/bet/falsification-condition shape (Lecture 3, Section 4) — you're reverse-engineering their bet from the outside, so say explicitly where you're inferring.

### Part 5 — If you were the PM

- In one paragraph: given everything above, what's the single highest-leverage thing you'd investigate or change next, and what metric would tell you if you were right? This should read like a real strategic bet, not a wish list.

---

## Milestones

- **Milestone 1 (45 min):** Pick the product, gather evidence (screenshots, review excerpts, pricing/changelog history) into `evidence/`.
- **Milestone 2 (45 min):** Parts 1–2 — role and users/value.
- **Milestone 3 (45 min):** Part 3 — lifecycle diagnosis with cited evidence.
- **Milestone 4 (45 min):** Parts 4–5 — VVF+U on a real bet, and your own recommendation.

---

## Rules

- **Every factual claim about the product's history, pricing, or evolution needs a source** in `evidence/` — a screenshot, a link, a dated quote. Claims about your own experience using it don't need external sourcing, but say when you're speaking from personal use versus research.
- **State your interpretation explicitly wherever the source data is ambiguous** — this mirrors every exercise and challenge this week; it's the core PM skill, not an afterthought.
- **The JTBD statement (Part 2) must pass the disguised-feature-request test** from Exercise 1 — no product or feature names inside it.

---

## Rubric

| Criterion | Weight | "Great" looks like |
|-----------|------:|--------------------|
| Role analysis (Part 1) | 10% | Reasons from observable evidence, honest about what's inferred |
| Users/job/value (Part 2) | 25% | JTBD statement passes the feature-request test; value prop is specific and checkable |
| Lifecycle diagnosis (Part 3) | 25% | Three+ independent, cited pieces of evidence; engages a competing interpretation |
| VVF+U + strategic bet (Part 4) | 25% | All four lenses reasoned through against a real, named change; bet has a falsification condition |
| Your recommendation (Part 5) | 15% | Reads as a real bet with a named metric, not a wish list |

---

## Reflection (`notes.md`, ~200 words)

1. Which part of the teardown was hardest to do from the outside, without insider access? What would you have needed to be more confident?
2. Did your lifecycle diagnosis (Part 3) match your gut instinct about the product going in, or did the evidence surprise you?
3. If you had to bet your own money on whether your Part 5 recommendation is right, how confident would you actually be — and what's the cheapest way to find out?
4. What's one thing about this product you now understand that you didn't before this week's frameworks existed for you?

## Why this matters

This teardown is the shape of a huge amount of real product work — sizing up a competitor, evaluating an acquisition target, onboarding onto a product you didn't build, or auditing your own team's roadmap with fresh eyes. Every framework from this week — role, JTBD, value proposition, lifecycle, VVF+U, and the strategic-bet shape — comes back constantly for the rest of the course, starting immediately with Week 2's user research, which is how you'd actually go verify everything you had to infer here from the outside.
