# Week 6 Challenges

Two open-ended problems. Unlike the exercises, there is no answer key to check your SQL against line-for-line — the point is to run a real investigation (Challenge 1) and make a defensible design call (Challenge 2), then show your work. Budget ~60–90 minutes each.

| # | Challenge | Difficulty | Practices |
|--:|-----------|------------|-----------|
| 1 | [Diagnose a Sudden Funnel Drop in SQL](./challenge-01-diagnose-a-funnel-drop.md) | Medium–Hard | Funnel-by-cohort SQL, anomaly hunting (Lecture 2) |
| 2 | [Design a Metric Tree for a Real Product](./challenge-02-design-a-metric-tree.md) | Medium | North Star selection, metric-tree decomposition (Lecture 1) |

## How these are graded

Challenge 1 *does* have a right answer buried in the seed data — a specific week, a specific funnel step, a specific magnitude — but there's no answer key file, because the entire point is that you find it the way a real PM would: by querying, not by being told where to look. Challenge 2 has no single right answer at all; the rubric in that file describes what separates a defensible tree from a hand-wavy one.

Both challenges reward the same habit this whole course has been building: **state your interpretation, cite the specific number that supports it, and name at least one alternative explanation you considered and ruled out.** A confident conclusion with no ruled-out alternatives is a weaker submission than a slightly more hedged one that shows its work.
