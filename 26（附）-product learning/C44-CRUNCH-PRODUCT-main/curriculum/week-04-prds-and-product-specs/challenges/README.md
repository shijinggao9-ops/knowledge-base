# Week 4 — Challenges

Two open-ended problems. Unlike the exercises, these have **no single right answer** — the skill is judgment, not recall. Do them after the three exercises.

1. **[Challenge 1 — Trim a Bloated PRD to Its Non-Goals](challenge-01-trim-a-bloated-prd.md)** — a real-looking, six-goal, scope-creeping draft PRD lands in your inbox. Cut it down to something buildable in one sprint, and write the non-goals section that makes the cut stick. *(~90 min.)*
2. **[Challenge 2 — Turn an Ambiguous Ask into a Buildable Spec](challenge-02-spec-an-ambiguous-request.md)** — an executive drops a two-sentence Slack message asking for a feature. You turn it into a real spec skeleton with stories, acceptance criteria starters, and the clarifying questions you'd actually need answered first. *(~90 min.)*

## How these are judged

There's no answer key with a single correct PRD. Instead, each challenge tells you what a *strong* submission looks like. You're being graded on:

- **Judgment under ambiguity** — did you make a defensible call, or did you freeze / guess wildly?
- **Explicit trade-offs** — did you say what you cut and why, not just present a shorter document with no explanation?
- **Traceability to a goal** — does everything you kept in scope trace back to a stated goal, and everything you cut have a stated reason?
- **Buildability** — could an engineer actually start work from what you produced, or is it still too vague to size?

Keep your work in `challenge-01.md` / `challenge-02.md` with your reasoning made explicit, not just your final artifact. The reasoning is the point — a good final PRD with no visible judgment behind it teaches you (and your reviewer) nothing about *how* you got there.
