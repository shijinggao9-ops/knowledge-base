# Week 5 — Challenges

Two open-ended problems. Unlike the exercises, these have **no single right answer** — the skill is judgment and communication under real conflict, not recall. Do them after the three exercises, once you have working RICE, Kano, and roadmap output to draw on.

1. **[Challenge 1 — Resolve a Sales-vs-Engineering Roadmap Conflict](challenge-01-resolve-stakeholder-conflict.md)** — a Sales VP and an Engineering lead each show up with "data" that supports a different priority order. Mediate it in writing, with numbers. *(~90 min.)*
2. **[Challenge 2 — Expose a Gamed RICE Score](challenge-02-expose-a-gamed-rice-score.md)** — a colleague's RICE spreadsheet-turned-SQL-query has a pet feature scored suspiciously high. Audit it, find exactly what was inflated, and correct the ranking. *(~90 min.)*

## How these are judged

There's no answer key with a single correct ranking. Instead, each challenge tells you what a *strong* submission looks like. You're being graded on:

- **Correctness of reasoning** — do your numbers actually support your conclusion, or did you pick a conclusion and back into numbers?
- **Explicit assumptions** — did you write down every judgment call, the way Weeks 1–4 taught you to?
- **Fairness to the other side** — in Challenge 1 especially, does your written mediation take the losing stakeholder's concern seriously, or does it just declare a winner?
- **Auditability** — in Challenge 2 especially, could a third person read your writeup and verify your correction themselves, using only the numbers you show your work with?

Keep your work in `challenge-01.md` / `challenge-02.md`, with your queries **and** your written reasoning. The reasoning is the point — a correct ranking with no explanation is an incomplete answer, even if the number is right.
