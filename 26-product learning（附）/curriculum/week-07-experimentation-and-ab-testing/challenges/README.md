# Week 7 — Challenges

Two open-ended problems. Unlike the exercises, these have **no single right answer** — the skill is judgment under real constraints, not recall of a formula. Do them after the three exercises.

1. **[Challenge 1 — Rescue an underpowered test](challenge-01-rescue-an-underpowered-test.md)** — the Stuck Task Alerts experiment from this week's seed data came back statistically inconclusive. Diagnose exactly why, and recommend what Loopline should actually do next. *(~90 min.)*
2. **[Challenge 2 — Design a test where you cannot randomize](challenge-02-design-a-test-without-randomization.md)** — Loopline wants to test a pricing change it legally and practically cannot A/B test the normal way. Design a rigorous alternative. *(~90 min.)*

## How these are judged

There's no answer key with a single correct number. Instead, each challenge tells you what a *strong* submission looks like. You're being graded on:

- **Correctness of diagnosis** — did you correctly identify *why* the situation is hard, using the right vocabulary from this week's lectures (power, MDE, unit of randomization, SUTVA, diff-in-diff, switchback)?
- **Realistic trade-offs** — a strong answer names the cost of each option (time, risk, precision) instead of picking one and pretending it's free.
- **A clear recommendation** — "it depends" is not a deliverable. Pick a path, defend it, and say what you'd need to be wrong about to change your mind.

Keep your work in `challenge-01.md` / `challenge-02.md` with your calculations **and** your written reasoning. The reasoning is the point — anyone can run a formula; the judgment is in knowing which one to run and what to do with the answer.
