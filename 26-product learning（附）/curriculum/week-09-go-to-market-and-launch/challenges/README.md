# Week 9 Challenges — Overview

Two challenges. Both are harder and more open-ended than the exercises — there is no single correct answer, and both are judged on the quality of your reasoning, not on matching a key.

| # | Challenge | Scenario | Time |
|--:|-----------|----------|-----:|
| 1 | [challenge-01-plan-a-risky-launch.md](challenge-01-plan-a-risky-launch.md) | A genuinely risky new feature — AI Task Summaries — needs a full launch plan from scratch | 1.5h |
| 2 | [challenge-02-recover-from-a-bad-launch.md](challenge-02-recover-from-a-bad-launch.md) | The Cascade Freight permission bug from Lecture 3 just happened. Write the recovery plan, live | 1.5h |

## How to work these

- Challenge 1 is a **forward-looking** planning exercise: no launch has happened yet, and your job is to design the tiers, channels, and kill criteria before anything ships.
- Challenge 2 is a **backward-looking** incident-response exercise: something has already gone wrong, and your job is to write the plan that gets the launch back on track without repeating the mistake.
- Both draw on everything from Lectures 1–3 and Exercises 1–3. If you're stuck, that's usually a sign to re-read a specific lecture section rather than guess.
- A written plan with a stated, defensible assumption beats a vague plan with no assumptions every time. Say what you decided and why.
