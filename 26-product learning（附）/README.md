# C44 · Crunch Product

> A free, open-source 12-week course to build products from discovery to launch with data and AI — user research, PRDs, prioritization, roadmaps, product analytics in SQL, experimentation, and AI features.

[![License: GPL v3](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-GPL%20v3-blue.svg)](LICENSE)

[![Built in the open](https://img.shields.io/badge/built-in%20the%20open-2463EB.svg)](https://github.com/CODECRUNCHWORLDWIDE)

C44 is a Tier-1 role course, sized long (12 weeks) because it goes all the way — from "what does a PM actually do?" to leading a product from zero to one with real analytics and shipped AI features. It leans on [C33 Crunch SQL](../C33-CRUNCH-SQL/) for the analytics weeks and pairs with [C27 Crunch Data](../C27-CRUNCH-DATA/) and [C5 Crunch AI & Data Science](../C5-CRUNCH-AI-DATA-SCIENCE/) — this course makes you the person who decides *what* to build and can prove it with data.

## Standards & equivalency

> C44 stands in for a university product-management course, and for the research-and-evaluation half of a human-computer interaction course.

**University equivalent.** Two of them, and they are claimed differently. **Product Management** — `ISM 4930`, `MGT 4570`, `ENGIN 183D`. Coverage: **full**. **Human-Computer Interaction** — `CIS 4930`, `CS 3750`, `CS 147`. Coverage: **partial**.

Partial has a precise meaning against the HCI claim, and it is not "most of it". Two things are true. First, C44 claims the **research and evaluation** half of an HCI course — needfinding, non-leading interviews, unbiased survey design, synthesis, flow analysis, critique, accessibility trade-offs and iteration from evidence. It does not claim the making half: sketching, wireframing, interactive prototyping and interface implementation are not taught here, and the ledger already points that half at a different course. Second, inside the half it does claim, the **usability-study method** is taught lighter than an HCI section teaches it — that is the gap the ledger records in `stillToAdd`, and the three rows marked `lighter` below say exactly which parts and how far short they fall.

C44 carries no credit, no transcript entry, no accreditation and no proctored exam. The equivalence is one of **content and skill**: every outcome below is taught here at the same depth or deeper except where a row says otherwise, and every one of them points at a week that assigns work on it — an exercise, a challenge, homework, a quiz item or the mini-project. What a registrar records is not something an open repository can give you.

The three `lighter` rows are the ledger's declared gap, stated exactly. Week 8 teaches Nielsen's ten heuristics and assigns a heuristic pass with 0–4 severity ratings, but the learner runs it alone; the multi-evaluator protocol an HCI section drills — several people evaluating independently, then reconciling and aggregating — is described in the lecture and never assigned. Week 8 assigns a five-participant think-aloud test with written task success criteria, but the moderator training, the pilot session and the concurrent-versus-retrospective choice an HCI section spends weeks on are not taught. Task analysis is not taught at all: flow mapping in Week 8 is the nearest thing on disk, and it is a coarser instrument.

**The industry bar.** What an employer expects of somebody paid to do this work, and where this course makes the learner do it. **C44's deliverables are documents, queries and decisions rather than a running application** — a brief, a PRD, a roadmap, an analysis, a launch plan, a spec. Where the industry clause assumes a codebase, the row below says what the equivalent practice is in the medium the deliverables actually use, and says plainly where the course does not do the thing at all.

## What you will be able to do at the end of 12 weeks

- **Run discovery like a pro:** plan and run user interviews, surveys, and usability tests, and turn raw signal into validated problems — not feature requests.
- **Frame the right problem:** size opportunities, write problem statements and JTBD, and kill bad ideas before they cost a sprint.
- **Write specs teams ship from:** PRDs, user stories, acceptance criteria, and edge cases that engineering and design can build without a meeting per line.
- **Prioritize with a defensible method:** RICE, weighted scoring, Kano, and cost-of-delay — and a roadmap that survives contact with reality.
- **Answer product questions in SQL:** funnels, retention cohorts, activation, and DAU/WAU/MAU straight from the event tables — never a spreadsheet as a database.
- **Run trustworthy experiments:** design an A/B test, size the sample, read significance honestly, and avoid the traps that fake a win.
- **Collaborate with design and engineering:** critique flows, reason about trade-offs, and drive a launch from spec to GA.
- **Price, grow, and monetize:** pricing models, packaging, growth loops, and the North Star metric that actually drives the business.
- **Ship AI features responsibly:** scope LLM product work, write evals, design for failure and cost, and know when *not* to use a model.
- **Lead zero to one:** take a product from a blank page to a launched, measured v1 — the capstone.

## How to navigate a week

Every week folder holds the same structure:

- **`README.md`** — the week overview + how the pieces fit + the week's goal.
- **`lecture-notes/`** — 3 lectures (~2 hrs each), the conceptual core.
- **`exercises/`** — 3 short, guided reps against real artifacts and data.
- **`challenges/`** — 2 open-ended problems with no single right answer.
- **`mini-project/`** — one build that ties the week together.
- **`homework.md`**, **`quiz.md`**, **`resources.md`** — practice, self-check, and further reading.

## Stack

PostgreSQL (16+) as the primary analytics engine (SQLite for zero-setup practice), with Python + pandas for deeper analysis and experiment math. Product data — events, funnels, cohorts, experiment logs — is stored and queried in **SQL, never a spreadsheet**; spreadsheets are a presentation surface only (and are taught in [C41 Crunch Excel](../C41-CRUNCH-EXCEL/)). You'll also use plain Markdown for PRDs and a seed event dataset shipped with the course.
